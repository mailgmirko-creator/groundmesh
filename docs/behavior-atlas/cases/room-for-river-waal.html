Evidence trail

1. National flood-risk purpose

Rijkswaterstaat describes Room for the River as a national flood-risk reduction programme using measures such as moving dikes inland, creating flood channels and lowering floodplains.

“It aims to reduce the risk of flooding by giving rivers more room to flood.”

Rijkswaterstaat · official programme record

Check the Rijkswaterstaat source

2. Measured Waal / Nijmegen flood-safety outcome

The European Climate-ADAPT case study reports that the main dike at Lent was moved 350 metres inland and that the completed intervention reduced river water height by 35 cm.

European Climate-ADAPT / European Environment Agency

Check the Climate-ADAPT case study

3. Stakeholder participation

The same case study records information meetings and interactive workshops with local stakeholders and describes broad participation as helping address much of the recorded doubt and opposition.

European Climate-ADAPT / European Environment Agency

4. Concentrated land burden

The Nijmegen intervention required land expropriation and compensation schemes, concentrating some project burdens on affected landholders and users.

“This involved expropriation of land and compensation schemes.”

European Climate-ADAPT / European Environment Agency

5. Programme-level outcomes and coordination

OECD describes the wider 2006–2018 programme as approximately EUR 2.3 billion, strongly coordinated across levels of government, and associated with improved flood safety, water-retention capacity and biodiversity.

“the programme significantly improved flood safety, increased water retention capacity, and enhanced biodiversity”

OECD · programme summary

Check the OECD source

What complicates the interpretation?

  • Programme-level flood and ecological benefits do not show how benefits were distributed to each household or landholder.
  • Participation and multi-level coordination support a cooperation pattern, but do not erase the coercive reality of expropriation where land was required.
  • The current record does not quantify whether compensation matched each affected party's material and non-material losses.
  • This case does not attempt a complete lifecycle ecological, land-value or maintenance-cost analysis.
